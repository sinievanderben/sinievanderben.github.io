How do concepts live inside language models?

I am a PhD student in mechanistic interpretability at ETH Zürich (IVIA Lab). I study how concepts are represented inside VLMs, and how reliably we can observe them, manipulate them and use them. I also work on how much our interpretability measurements can be trusted in the first place.

01 Research

My research asks how a concept, such as an emotion, is represented inside a language model, and whether what we read out of it actually matters for what the model does. I organise this around three questions:

  • Observe What is the geometry of a concept in the model? Emotion representations in open-weight LLMs
  • Manipulate Can we predict which directions are steerable before steering? Steering and covariance structure
  • Use Does an internal state drive behaviour, or only the expressed output? Internal vs. external emotion

Why this matters for AI safety

As language models take on more consequential tasks, we need to check what is happening inside them, not only what they say. If we can locate a concept, show that it causally drives behaviour, and know how far our measurements can be trusted, we get tools to detect and correct unwanted internal states before they surface in a model's output. Emotions are a useful test case: they are abstract, they appear across models, and what a model expresses can differ from what it represents internally. That gap is exactly what oversight needs to be able to see.
